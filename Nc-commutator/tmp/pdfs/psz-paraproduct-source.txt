Let (M, τ ) be a finite von Neumann algebra equipped with a normalized faithful trace and let A be a
nest of order type N. The nest algebra is defined by Hr∞ (A) = {x ∈ M : ex = exe, e ∈ A}, and the related
noncommutative Hardy space Hrp (A) is the completion of Hr∞ (A) in Lp (M). In the present paper, we make
a connection between Hrp (A) and certain noncommutative martingale Hardy space via showing that tri-
angular truncation is a concrete martingale transform. We include several related results for noncom-
mutative nest Hardy spaces in the spirit of recent development of noncommutative martingale theory.




1 Introduction
In his seminal paper [1], Arveson introduced the notion of subdiagonal algebras. Let M be a von
Neumann algebra equipped with a normal faithful tracial state τ . Let D be a von Neumann subalgebra of
M, and let E : M → D be the unique normal faithful conditional expectation such that τ ◦ E = τ . A weak-
∗-closed subalgebra H∞ (M) of M is called a finite subdiagonal algebra of M with respect to E (or D ) if

   (i) {x + y∗ : x, y ∈ H∞ (M)} is weak-∗-dense in M;
  (ii) E (xy) = E (x)E (y) for all x, y ∈ H∞ (M); and
 (iii) {x : x, x∗ ∈ H∞ (M)} = D .

   The subalgebra D is then called the diagonal of H∞ (M). According to [15], H∞ (M) is automatically
maximal in the sense that it is contained in no proper subdiagonal algebra with respect to E other than
itself. For 0 < p < ∞, the Hardy space Hp (M) is defined as the closure of H∞ (M) in Lp (M). If M = L∞ (D)
where D is the open unit disc in the complex plane, then H∞ (M) = H∞ (D) is the algebra of analytic
and bounded functions on D and the diagonal subalgebra D is given by C; moreover, Hp (M) = Hp (D) is
the classical analytic Hardy space. Hence, Hp (M)’s are noncommutative extensions of classical Hardy
spaces on the unit disc D. On the other hand, the triangular algebra studied in Kadison and Singer’s
paper [30] (see also [37]) provides an important noncommutative example for H∞ (M). Many results
on the classical Hardy spaces in the unit disc have been extended to the noncommutative setting. For
instance, we refer the reader to [3, 8, 19, 32–34, 42, 47, 48, 53], and the survey papers [7, 45] for more
information and references.
   Parallel to the development of the theory of noncommutative Hardy spaces, the noncommutative
martingale Hardy space theory also got rapid development recently. Indeed, in 1997, Pisier and Xu
[43] introduced suitable definition of noncommmutative martingale Hardy spaces and established

   In the commutative setting, the Hardy space Hp (D) in harmonic analysis has deep connection with
martingales. For example, Maurey [35] proved the famous result that H1 (D) is isomorphic to a dyadic
martingale Hardy space. A natural question is whether there exists some connection between the above
noncommutative Hardy spaces and noncommutative martingales. We answer this question positively in the
sense of noncommutative Hardy spaces associated with nest algebras. Let A be a nest of order type N,
that is (see, e.g., [2, Example 2.2]),

                                                  A = {ek , 1}k≥0 ,

where 1 denotes the identity of M, and {ek }k≥0 is a sequence of increasing projections in M such that
e0 = 0 and ∨k ek = 1. The nest algebra Hr∞ (M, A) (shortly Hr∞ (A)) with respect to A is defined by (see, e.g.,
[2,p. 11] or [12,p. 22])

                                    Hr∞ (A) = {x ∈ M : xe = exe,           ∀e ∈ A}.

If M ⊂ B(H) for some Hilbert space H and each ek (H) has dimension k, then the nest algebra Hr∞ (A) is a
triangular algebra in the sense of [30]. Moreover, the nest algebra Hr∞ (A) is a finite subdiagonal algebra
with the diagonal algebra D given by Hr∞ (A) ∩ Hc∞ (A), where Hc∞ (A) = {x∗ : x ∈ Hr∞ (A)}. For 0 < p < ∞,
the noncommutative Hardy space Hrp (A) is the completion of Hr∞ (A) with respect to the Lp (M) norm.
The symbol “r” appears in Hrp (A) because later we would like to compare it with row martingale Hardy
spaces (see, e.g., Proposition 3.7). Note that the Hardy space Hrp (A) can be also defined by the triangular
truncation given as follows:

    After making the connection between noncommutative nest Hardy spaces and martingales, we study
related results in the setting noncommutative nest Hardy spaces by the techniques imported from the

    In Section 3, we show that the triangular truncation T defined above is actually a concrete martingale

(shortly Hr∞ (A)) with respect to A is defined by

                                      Hr∞ (A) = {x ∈ M : xe = exe,         ∀e ∈ A}.

Similarly, define

                                      Hc∞ (A) = {x ∈ M : ex = exe,         ∀e ∈ A}.

Note that x ∈ Hr∞ (A) if and only x∗ ∈ Hc∞ (A). For 0 < p < ∞, the noncommutative Hardy space Hrp (A)
(resp. Hcp (A)) is the completion of Hr∞ (A) (resp. Hc∞ (A)) with respect to the Lp (M) norm.

                                                       A = {ek , 1}k≥0 ,
